April 20, 2026

Export Still Images for Strava, Posts, and Thumbnails

You do not always need a full video render to share a ride update. Sometimes one well-timed frame says everything.

Export a single polished frame

Image export lets you save the current editor frame as a shareable image with your active widgets already applied.

That is useful for:

  • Strava ride posts
  • Instagram carousels
  • YouTube thumbnails
  • quick previews for friends or sponsors

It is one of the fastest ways to publish something right after a ride because you can skip a full video render entirely.

What makes it practical

The editor supports common output sizes like 720p, 1080p, and 4K, so you can choose between speed and detail.

You can also pick the format that suits the job:

  • PNG when you want a crisp lossless export
  • JPG when you want a lighter file that is easy to post or send quickly

For repeat use, Quick Export saves the current frame immediately using your existing preferences. That makes it easy to scrub, click once, and keep moving.

If you often export images from the same kind of project, your preferred settings can carry over so you do not have to reconfigure the export every time.

Nice extras for ride sharing

If you export a JPG, the workflow can also include GPS-related metadata for people who like keeping their snapshots tied to the route.

That is a nice quality-of-life detail for galleries, ride recaps, and activity posts, but the main benefit is still speed: one frame, ready to share.

A simple sharing workflow

  1. Scrub to a frame that tells the story well.
  2. Check that the widgets are readable and the composition looks balanced.
  3. Open Export Image or use Quick Export.
  4. Choose your size and format if needed.
  5. Save the frame and drop it into your social post, activity log, or thumbnail workflow.

Why people use this often

Still images are much faster to make than a full video export, but they still carry the same telemetry context. For many creators, that makes image export the quickest way to publish something right after a ride.
